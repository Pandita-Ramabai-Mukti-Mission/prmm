---
title: Pandita Ramabai
---

Ramabai Dongre-Medhavi — a champion for the emancipation of women and a pioneer in education — opened a home for widows in the late 1800s, which gradually welcomed every needy soul who came to it and grew into Pandita Ramabai Mukti Mission. "Mukti" means freedom, liberation or salvation, and the mission continues to care for needy women and children irrespective of caste, creed, religion or status — among them orphans, destitute and differently-abled residents, and survivors of severe abuse, violence and trauma. The Mission's work is to accept, care for, and bring positive transformation to disadvantaged women and children, creating opportunities for them to become strong, contributing members of society.

Pandita Ramabai Mukti Mission, at Kedgaon in Pune district, is registered under the Societies Registration Act (XXX of 1950), the Bombay Public Trust Act of 1950, and the Women's and Children's Institutions Act of 1956. Its homes serve orphans and semi-orphans, elderly women, and girls and women with special needs, including a dedicated section for the blind with a Braille school, alongside a nursery and adoption facility.

Education was central to Ramabai's own life and to the mission from its founding: she was an exceptionally well-educated social reformer, and remains the only woman ever conferred the title "Pandita" for her command of Sanskrit. In 1919 she was honoured by the Emperor with the "Kaiser-E-Hind" award for her contribution to society.

The mission's community programs — mobile health clinics, adult literacy, vocational training, kindergartens and day-care centres, and expansion homes across India — extend this same work beyond Kedgaon, alongside an ongoing focus on women's and children's rights, equality, and the fight against abuse, violence and trafficking. As the mission's own "Shaping Lives" mark puts it: lives, no matter how broken or shattered, can be rebuilt, restored and renewed.
