---
title: About Mukti Mission
---

Founded in 1889 by Pandita Ramabai as a home for widows, Mukti Mission — "Mukti" meaning freedom, liberation and salvation — has grown into 14 ministries serving orphaned, destitute and vulnerable women and children, regardless of caste, creed or religion. Mukti has continued that work ever since Ramabai's death in 1922, now in its 130th-plus year, with a stated passion of "creating beauty out of ashes."

### Mukti's Vision

Christ Centred Home where destitute women and children irrespective of their background are accepted, cared for, transformed and empowered to be Salt &amp; Light in the society.

### Mukti's Mission

We seek, in the Spirit of Christ, to sow seeds of change in the lives of every individual under the care of Mukti Mission, skillfully counseling them to deal with the issues of life, initiating varied opportunities of healing, and shaping them prayerfully as models of God's kingdom for the society at large.

### Basis of Mukti

"The spirit of the Sovereign Lord is on me, because the Lord has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor. He has sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim freedom for the captives and release from darkness for the prisoners." — Isaiah 61:1, 4

### Why We Exist

Mukti exists to make a difference in the lives of:

- Orphans and special-needs women and children
- Widows, single parents, unwed mothers and childless couples
- People who are visually or physically challenged
- Children from the streets and slums
- Children of commercial sex workers
- Children affected by natural disaster
- Indigenous people groups
- The poor in the surrounding community, through medical care and a day-care center

### Character of Mukti

Mukti describes itself as Christ-centred, focused on destitute women and children, holistic and multi-faceted, continuing Pandita Ramabai's founding vision, and fully governed by Indians.

### Women's Leadership

Mukti was started by a woman, for women, in 1889. Its philosophy: reaching women in the community, affirming the rightful place of women in God's plan, and the conviction that women can change the destiny of the nation — summarized in one line the mission uses often: "if you empower a man, you empower none; if you empower a girl, you empower the nation."
